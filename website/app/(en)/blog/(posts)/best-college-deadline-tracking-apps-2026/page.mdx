Every approach to tracking college deadlines eventually runs into the same test: what happens when four courses' worth of assignments, exams, and readings all need to live in the same place at once. A method that works fine for one class in week two often falls apart by week ten, once quizzes, problem sets, project checkpoints, and a couple of midterms are all competing for the same calendar. Here's how the common approaches — and a specific set of apps built for this exact problem — hold up against that test, plus the framework we used to compare them and a quick guide to which one fits a given situation.

## The paper planner: flexible, but it has no memory

A paper planner or a spreadsheet works, but every date has to be transcribed by hand from every syllabus, and if a professor moves a due date, you have to notice and re-copy it yourself. There's also no cross-referencing built in — nothing stops "Essay 2" and "Problem Set 6" from looking identical on the page even though one is worth ten times as much of the final grade.

## Generic to-do apps remove the paper, not the work

A generic to-do app removes the paper but not the labor — it can hold a due date and a checkbox once you've entered it, but it has no idea what a syllabus is, no concept of grading weight, and no way to flag that three courses just landed exams in the same week. Entering a semester's worth of deadlines by hand into Reminders, Todoist, or a similar app still means reading every syllabus line by line and typing each date in yourself, and redoing that work every time a due date shifts. Neither approach is wrong, exactly — they just put the entire syllabus-to-calendar conversion labor on the student, every single time something changes.

## The four questions we measured against

A newer category of app reads the syllabus, or connects to the school's LMS, and builds the deadline list for you rather than asking you to type it in. Eight of them are covered below, and none of them work identically.

This comparison is published by Semora, one of the products included. It is a desk review of public product pages, pricing pages, help documentation, and store listings checked in August 2026; it is not a claim that we hands-on tested every product. Features and prices change, so verify a vendor's current documentation before choosing.

We compared all eight against four questions that predict whether an app holds up across a full semester rather than just a demo:

1. **Does it read a syllabus, or does it require an LMS connection (or manual entry) to get deadlines in at all?** Semora, DormWay, Shovel, and StudyFetch all do some form of AI syllabus parsing. Mindgrasp and Studley AI process uploaded material but don't parse a syllabus for deadlines. Taskade has no document-parsing pipeline for this. myHomework relies on manual entry or LMS import only.
2. **Is grade tracking included, or just a deadline list?** Semora and DormWay both include it — DormWay via a GPA/grade calculator with adjustable weighted categories — while the other six don't describe grade tracking as a core, confirmed feature.
3. **Is there a real, ongoing free tier, or just a trial before a subscription starts?** DormWay is entirely free with no paid tier. Semora's free tier (5 scans/month, 4 courses) has no time limit. Shovel's pricing page advertises a 7-day trial before billing starts, and Mindgrasp's official site advertises a trial rather than a standing free tier. StudyFetch and Studley AI report free tiers with meaningful caps rather than full functionality; myHomework's free version is ad-supported instead.
4. **Does it sync across your phone and a browser without extra setup?** Coverage varies widely — from Semora and StudyFetch's phone-plus-web setups, to DormWay and myHomework's broader multi-platform reach, to Shovel and Taskade's native iOS/Android apps alongside a web app.

The sections that follow take each app in turn, grouped by how it answers question one, because that is the answer that decides whether an app can do this job at all.

## Semora: the syllabus as the starting point

[Semora](/features) scans a syllabus by photo, PDF, drag-and-drop, or pasted text and extracts every assignment, exam, quiz, project, and reading with its due date, along with the grading scale, meeting times, and office hours — turning deadline tracking and grade tracking into the same free-tier feature. The free tier includes 5 syllabus scans per calendar month across up to 4 courses in one semester, with full deadline and grade tracking, and reminders 3 days, 1 day and the morning before each deadline included at no cost; calendar sync with .ics export is a Pro feature. The current Canvas connector uses a personal access token and should be used only where an institution permits third-party token connections. See the full [Semora vs DormWay](/compare/dormway) and [Semora vs Taskade](/compare/taskade) comparisons for more detail.

## DormWay: free, across three LMS platforms

[DormWay](/compare/dormway) pairs AI syllabus parsing with read-only sync across Canvas, Blackboard, and Moodle, merged into one semester timeline, and is currently free with no paid tier — its own site and App Store listing state "no paywalls" and "no credit card." It also includes a GPA/grade calculator with adjustable weighted categories, an "Ace" AI assistant that answers policy questions with citations back to the syllabus, and a per-course "Intelligence" tab showing a difficulty rating and weekly hour estimate. It runs on web, iPhone, iPad, and Mac, but has no Android app, per DormWay's own blog.

## Shovel: available time as the constraint

[Shovel](/compare/shovel) turns a PDF syllabus or a direct LMS connection (Canvas, Brightspace, Moodle, or Google Classroom) into a time-blocked study schedule, weighing available time against estimated time per task. Distinct features include "The Cushion™" predictive conflict alerts, reading-time estimators, streak-based motivation tracking, and free supplementary "how to study" courses. LMS sync auto-refreshes roughly every 24 hours, and it has native iOS and Android apps. Shovel's official pages did not agree when checked August 9, 2026: its [pricing page](https://shovelapp.io/pricing/) showed a 7-day trial followed by $9.79/month (with $19.99 also displayed) or $39/year, while its navigation-linked [buy page](https://shovelapp.io/buy/) showed $33/month paid monthly or $16/month paid annually. Confirm the price at checkout.

## StudyFetch: an AI tutor that wants your material first

[StudyFetch](/compare/studyfetch) includes syllabus-to-calendar scanning as one feature inside a broader AI study platform built around its Spark.E tutor, which answers questions only from a student's own uploaded course material (slides, PDFs, notes, photos, video, or audio) rather than the open internet. Around that core, it auto-generates flashcards, quizzes, exam simulations, essay feedback, AI-narrated "podcast" summaries, and explainer videos, and a Live Lecture Assistant records class audio into structured notes. It documents an LTI 1.3 integration with Canvas, Blackboard, Schoology, D2L Brightspace, and Google Classroom, deployed at the institution level rather than by an individual student. Reported pricing (via third-party review sites, not confirmed on StudyFetch's own page) includes a free tier limited to 10 Spark.E chats, 1 study set, and 2 uploads, a Base tier around $7.99/month, a Premium tier around $11.99/month, a semester bundle around $49.99, and an annual plan around $99.99.

## Mindgrasp and Studley AI: complements, not replacements

Neither of these parses a syllabus, which is not a failing so much as a different product. Both sit downstream of the deadline problem: you already know what is coming, and you want the material turned into something you can practice with.

[Mindgrasp](/compare/mindgrasp) doesn't parse a syllabus for deadlines — it turns an uploaded document, video, or lecture recording into notes, flashcards, and a quiz, making it a complement to a deadline tracker rather than a replacement for one. You can upload or link a PDF, DOCX, PowerPoint, MP3/MP4, YouTube video, or web article, or record a lecture live, and Mindgrasp generates AI notes, a summary, flashcards, a quiz, and an "AI Tutor" chat for that content. A higher Scholar/Premium tier adds an "AI math expert," and there's a Chrome extension for capturing content from the browser. Mindgrasp states compatibility with Canvas, Blackboard, and Panopto, though that appears to cover importing files rather than parsing a syllabus for deadlines. With the Yearly selector active on Mindgrasp's [official plan picker](https://app.mindgrasp.ai/pick-plan) on August 9, 2026, Basic showed $5.99/month billed $71.88 once per year, Scholar $8.99/month billed $107.88 once per year, and Premium $10.99/month billed $131.88 once per year. The official site also advertises a free trial; confirm the offer and any monthly-billing prices at checkout.

[Studley AI](/compare/studley-ai) is a materials-first app similar in spirit to Mindgrasp: you upload PDFs, lecture slides, YouTube videos, article links, or a photo of handwritten notes, and its AI converts that into flashcards, multiple-choice quizzes, fill-in-the-blank exercises, written-response tests, and an AI tutor. A "Solve" feature lets you photograph a homework problem for a step-by-step explanation, and a podcast feature turns material into AI-narrated audio, with progress tracked across four mastery levels. No syllabus-parsing or LMS integration appears anywhere in its public materials. Per its own App Store listing, Studley AI has been downloaded 460,000+ times with a 4.74/5 rating from 31,000+ reviews, and the company states over 1,000,000 users — self-reported figures, not independently verified. Its free tier is 1 study set per day, with an Unlimited plan reported at $12.88/month or $97.76/year via third-party review coverage.

## Taskade and myHomework: the two opposite extremes

These two bracket the category from either end — one a general-purpose workspace that was never built for coursework, the other a planner that deliberately does nothing but plan.

[Taskade](/compare/taskade) is a general-purpose AI workspace with seven interchangeable project views (List, Board, Calendar, Table, Mind Map, Gantt, Org Chart) sharing the same underlying data, built for teams and freelancers rather than students — there's no native pipeline for reading a syllabus PDF into dated assignments, and no LMS integrations are evident in its feature set. Its differentiators are the ability to build custom AI agents trained on your own docs, deploy multi-agent "teams," and run automations — useful broadly, but none of it purpose-built for a class schedule. Taskade's own pricing page lists a free tier (1 user, 3 apps, one-time AI credits), Pro at $10/month billed annually, Business at $25/month, and Max at $100/month.

[myHomework Student Planner](/compare/myhomework) takes the most manual approach of the group: you enter classes and due dates yourself, or import assignments from Canvas, D2L, Google Classroom, Blackboard, Schoology, and other platforms, with a premium account auto-updating the planner as new assignments appear. There's no syllabus photo or PDF parsing at all. What it does have is broad platform reach — iOS, Android, Mac, Windows, Chrome, and Kindle Fire — plus 60+ customizable themes, homework widgets, color-coded courses, and reminder notifications. The base version is free with ads; an ad-free premium tier is reported around $4.99/year, adding file attachments, external calendar access, planner sharing, and LMS imports. Worth noting: the company's current business focus has shifted toward a K-12 digital hall-pass product, though the original Student Planner app remains separately available and was still updated as recently as early 2025.

## Which one fits you

- If your school uses Blackboard or Moodle specifically, alongside or instead of Canvas → [DormWay](/compare/dormway)'s read-only sync covers all three, merged into one timeline.
- If you want time-blocked scheduling — weighing available time against estimated time-per-task — as the core feature rather than an add-on → [Shovel](/compare/shovel).
- If you want an AI tutor that can ingest your own lecture recordings, slides, and notes directly, with syllabus scanning as a secondary convenience → [StudyFetch](/compare/studyfetch).
- If you just need flashcards, a quiz, or notes generated from a specific uploaded document, video, or recording, and don't need a deadline calendar at all → [Mindgrasp](/compare/mindgrasp) or [Studley AI](/compare/studley-ai), depending on whether you also want a photo-based homework solver (Studley AI) or an AI math expert on a higher tier (Mindgrasp).
- If you want a lightweight, broadly cross-platform planner (including Mac, Windows, or Kindle Fire) and don't need AI syllabus parsing → [myHomework Student Planner](/compare/myhomework).
- If you're managing a team or project outside of coursework entirely → [Taskade](/compare/taskade)'s general-purpose views and custom AI agents are built for that, not for a syllabus specifically.
- If you want the syllabus-to-calendar step done automatically, with grade tracking in the same free tier → that's the gap Semora is built to fill, detailed below.
